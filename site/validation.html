Validation methodology

How the savings and safety numbers were produced, what was excluded, and where selection fell short.

Design

  1. Replay. For each project, 200 consecutive commits are each treated as the change introduced over the commit before it.
  2. Select. Blastradius selects tests for that change using an index built on the earlier commit.
  3. Compare with ground truth. A would-miss is a test that caught a real regression or an injected mutant but that selection chose not to run.
  4. Mutate. On the same run, bounded mutation validation injects synthetic faults into the head commit and checks whether selected tests catch them. A killing test passed on head, failed on the mutant, and stayed failed on confirmation. Counts are per mutant.

“Skipped” is a share of test executions in the replay, not measured CI wall-clock savings.

Exclusions, stated plainly

  • Excluded pairs are ones the harness could not measure; they count neither for nor against selection.
  • httpcomponents-client: 11 pairs hit the build timeout and 1 hit a Surefire report with an XML-illegal character (since fixed). TestTlsHandshakeTimeout#testTimeout was excluded as flaky under parallel load, which is what brings its would-miss to 0.
  • jsoup: all 10 exclusions exceeded the 40-minute mutation-build timeout.
  • shenyu: HttpClientPluginConfigurationTest was excluded as flaky.
  • commons-io: an initially recorded would-miss on URIOriginTest (a live-network fixture) did not reproduce in a plain build; the pair now fails under the validator harness for a cause not yet identified and is excluded, alongside 11 timeout pairs. The would-miss of 0 is therefore not a confirmed miss, but it is not a clean zero either.

What fell short

  • commons-io: 1,011 of 31,648 killing executions (3.2%) were skipped across 42 commit pairs, on the diff-targeted or whole-tree-fallback path. Every one of the 850 caught mutants still had at least one killing test selected, so this is partial under-selection, not a blind spot. It has not been re-verified at lower concurrency; treat it as provisional.
  • jsoup: the recorded run first skipped 5,808 killing executions, 5,682 from 12 QueryParser mutations and 126 from two Normalizer mutations. After the bounded two-hop static-invocation rule was added, targeted replays of all 14 mutation identities selected all 16,642 killing executions. This is a string-dispatched-API case, the class of gap that remains hardest.

Known limits

  • Reflection, generated calls, incomplete instrumentation, and static-invocation paths deeper than two edges are outside the evidence. Projects built around a string DSL (selector engines, expression languages, query parsers) should weigh this.
  • Kotlin inline functions force a full run; custom @file:JvmName facades are not mapped.
  • Results are specific to these four projects and windows. They do not prove universal safety.
  • Keep a regular (recommended: daily) full-suite run as the backstop.

Validate on your own history

The validator harness replays a project’s own commits in shadow mode and reports would-miss cases before you adopt the plugin.
